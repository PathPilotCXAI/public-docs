PathPilot uses role-based access control (RBAC) to ensure that every member of your team has exactly the access they need — no more, no less. When you invite a user to your workspace, you assign them one of four predefined roles. That role determines what they can view, create, edit, and manage across controls, evidence, workflows, integrations, and account settings. Choosing the right role for each person is a key part of your SOC 2 access control requirements and helps you maintain a clear, auditable record of who can do what in your compliance program.

## Roles Overview

PathPilot provides four roles, each designed for a distinct function in your compliance workflow.

**Admin** is the most privileged role and is intended for the people responsible for managing your PathPilot workspace. Admins have full access to every part of the platform: they can invite and remove users, assign roles, manage billing and subscriptions, configure integrations and SSO, create and edit controls, upload and manage evidence, build workflows, and view all audit logs. Most organizations should limit the number of Admins to a small group of trusted individuals.

**Editor** is the everyday working role for compliance practitioners and team members who actively contribute to your SOC 2 program. Editors can create and edit controls, upload evidence, manage workflows, and update task statuses. They cannot manage other users, change roles, access billing settings, or modify organization-level security configuration. This separation ensures that operational contributors can do their work without exposure to sensitive administrative functions.

**Auditor** is a read-only role designed specifically for external auditors, consultants, or internal audit teams who need to review your compliance posture without making any changes. Auditors can view all controls, evidence, and audit logs, and they can download reports and evidence packages. They cannot create, edit, or delete anything in the workspace. Granting an Auditor role to your external auditor during a SOC 2 assessment gives them the access they need while keeping your data protected.

**Viewer** is a lightweight read-only role for stakeholders who need visibility into your compliance status without needing the full detail available to Auditors. Viewers can browse controls and see high-level compliance status dashboards. They cannot view sensitive evidence, access audit logs, or make any modifications. This role is well-suited for executives, board members, or other internal stakeholders who want awareness without deep access.

## Assigning Roles

You assign a role to a user at the time you invite them to PathPilot. When filling out the invitation form under **Settings → Users → Invite User**, you select the role from a dropdown before sending the invite.

If you need to change a user's role after they've joined — for example, promoting an Editor to an Admin, or downgrading a former Admin who has changed responsibilities — go to **Settings → Users**, find the user in the list, click the **three-dot menu** next to their name, and select **Edit Role**. Choose the new role from the dropdown and save. The change takes effect immediately.

## Custom Roles

If the four built-in roles don't precisely match your organizational structure or compliance requirements, PathPilot offers custom role creation on the **Enterprise plan**. Custom roles let you define granular permission sets tailored to specific job functions — for example, a role that can upload evidence and manage workflows but cannot view audit logs.

To discuss custom roles and Enterprise plan pricing, reach out to the PathPilot team at [sales@pathpilot.com](mailto:sales@pathpilot.com). The team will work with you to design a permission model that fits your organization's needs.

<Note>
  Role changes take effect immediately upon saving. If a user is actively logged in when their role is changed, their permissions update in real time — they do not need to log out and back in. Downgrading a user's role will instantly remove their access to any features or data outside their new role's scope.
</Note>
