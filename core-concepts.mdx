Before you dive into configuring controls or connecting integrations, it helps to understand how PathPilot is structured. This page explains the six fundamental building blocks of PathPilot — workspaces, controls, evidence, workflows, audit logs, and roles — and how they work together to power your compliance program. Refer back here whenever you need a clear definition or want to understand how the pieces fit.

---

## Workspaces

A **workspace** is an isolated environment that contains everything belonging to one organization or team: controls, evidence, workflows, members, integrations, and settings. Think of it as your organization's private compliance hub inside PathPilot.

When you first sign up, PathPilot walks you through creating your workspace and selecting a compliance framework (such as SOC2). Everything you configure — your controls, your evidence artifacts, your audit history — lives inside that workspace and is not visible to other organizations.

You can create multiple workspaces if your organization manages compliance for separate business units or subsidiaries that need distinct audit trails. Each workspace operates independently with its own member list and permission settings.

---

## Controls

**Controls** are the individual security and compliance requirements your organization must satisfy to achieve and maintain SOC2 certification. Each control maps to one or more SOC2 Trust Service Criteria (TSC) — for example, CC6.1 (Logical and Physical Access Controls) falls under the Security category.

Every control in PathPilot has:

- **Status** — one of four states that reflects the current implementation health of the control:
  - `Not Started` — the control has been activated but no work has begun.
  - `In Progress` — the owner is actively working on implementing or documenting the control.
  - `Passing` — sufficient evidence exists and the control is considered satisfied.
  - `Failing` — the control has gaps, missing evidence, or has been flagged as non-compliant.
- **Owner** — a specific team member responsible for maintaining the control, uploading evidence, and resolving any issues.
- **Linked Evidence** — one or more artifacts that demonstrate the control is implemented (see [Evidence](#evidence) below).

PathPilot ships with a pre-built control library aligned to SOC2 Trust Service Criteria. You activate the controls relevant to your product and environment, rather than building them from scratch.

## Evidence

**Evidence** is any artifact that proves a control is implemented and operating effectively. During a SOC2 audit, your auditor will review evidence to verify that each control is not just documented on paper but actively enforced in your environment.

Evidence in PathPilot can take many forms:

- **Policy documents** — written policies such as your Access Control Policy, Incident Response Plan, or Business Continuity Plan.
- **Screenshots** — visual proof of configurations, dashboards, or system states.
- **API logs and system exports** — raw log data or configuration exports pulled from your infrastructure.
- **Automated test results** — output from security scans, penetration tests, or compliance checks.

You can add evidence to a control in two ways:

1. **Manual upload** — upload a file directly from your computer or paste an external URL.
2. **Automated collection** — connect an integration (GitHub, AWS, Slack, and others) and let PathPilot pull evidence automatically on a defined schedule or in response to events.

Evidence artifacts have a **renewal date**. PathPilot alerts control owners when evidence is approaching expiration so your controls never lapse into a failing state due to stale documentation.

## Workflows

**Workflows** are automated sequences that PathPilot executes to keep your compliance program running without manual intervention. A workflow can be triggered on a **schedule** (for example, every 30 days) or by an **event** (for example, when a new employee is onboarded or a GitHub branch protection rule changes).

A workflow can perform actions such as:

- Collecting evidence from a connected integration and linking it to the relevant control.
- Sending a notification to a control owner via Slack or email when evidence is due for renewal.
- Creating a remediation task and assigning it to the appropriate team member when a control moves into a `Failing` state.
- Running an automated check against your AWS or GitHub configuration and updating control status based on the result.

Workflows reduce the manual overhead of compliance maintenance and ensure that your controls remain up to date between audits — not just in the weeks before one.

<Info>
  You can build custom workflows using PathPilot's workflow editor, or start from one of the pre-built workflow templates in the **Workflows** library. Templates exist for common scenarios like quarterly access reviews, new-hire security training reminders, and vendor risk assessments.
</Info>

## Audit Logs

**Audit logs** are tamper-evident, append-only records of every action taken inside your PathPilot workspace. Every time a user activates a control, uploads evidence, changes a setting, invites a teammate, or modifies a workflow, PathPilot records the event with a timestamp, the identity of the actor, and the details of what changed.

Audit logs serve two critical purposes:

1. **Demonstrating change history during a SOC2 audit** — your auditor can review the audit log to verify that your compliance program was actively managed throughout the audit period, not assembled at the last minute.
2. **Internal accountability** — your security team can investigate incidents or anomalies by tracing exactly who did what and when inside PathPilot.

Audit logs cannot be edited or deleted by any user, including workspace Admins. This immutability is intentional — it ensures the integrity of your compliance record.

## Roles

PathPilot uses role-based access control to ensure that each team member has exactly the permissions they need — no more, no less. Every member of a workspace is assigned one of four roles:

- **Admin** — full access to the workspace. Admins can configure controls, manage integrations, edit workflows, invite or remove members, and adjust workspace-level settings.
- **Editor** — can activate and update controls, upload and manage evidence, and configure workflows, but cannot manage members or change workspace settings.
- **Auditor** — read access to all controls, evidence, and audit logs, plus the ability to leave comments and flag issues for review. Auditors cannot modify controls or evidence. This role is typically used for external auditors or internal compliance reviewers.
- **Viewer** — read-only access to the compliance dashboard and control statuses. Viewers cannot take any action that changes data. Ideal for executives, board members, or external stakeholders who need visibility.

For a complete breakdown of what each role can and cannot do, see the [Permissions Reference](/account/permissions).
