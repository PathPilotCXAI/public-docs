A compliance platform is only as trustworthy as its access controls. PathPilot holds some of your organization's most sensitive materials — audit evidence, policy documents, control gaps, and integration credentials — and we take seriously the responsibility to ensure only the right people can see and act on that data. This page covers every mechanism PathPilot provides for controlling who gets in, what they can do, and how every action is recorded.

## Authentication Methods

PathPilot supports multiple authentication methods so you can match your organization's identity practices without compromising security.

**Email and password.** Users can sign in with a verified email address and a password that meets PathPilot's complexity requirements (minimum 12 characters, mixed case, at least one number and one special character). Passwords are hashed using bcrypt before storage and are never accessible in plaintext by PathPilot staff.

**Single Sign-On (SSO) via SAML 2.0.** PathPilot supports SAML 2.0 federation with any standards-compliant identity provider. Pre-built integrations are available for:
- Okta
- Microsoft Azure Active Directory (Azure AD / Entra ID)
- Google Workspace

SSO is available on Pro and Enterprise plans. When SSO is enforced for your organization, users cannot create local email/password credentials — all authentication flows through your identity provider. See the [SSO Configuration](#sso-configuration) section below for setup instructions.

**API key authentication.** Machine-to-machine access to PathPilot's API is authenticated using long-lived API keys. Keys are scoped to a specific role and can be revoked at any time from **Settings → API Keys**. All API key usage is recorded in your workspace's audit log.

## Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds a second verification step after password entry, making credential theft attacks significantly less effective. PathPilot supports two MFA methods:

- **TOTP (Time-Based One-Time Password).** Use any TOTP-compatible authenticator app — such as Google Authenticator, Authy, or 1Password — to generate a 6-digit code that refreshes every 30 seconds.
- **Hardware security keys (WebAuthn).** Register a FIDO2-compatible hardware key such as a YubiKey or Apple Touch ID. Hardware keys provide phishing-resistant authentication and are the strongest MFA option available.

**Enabling MFA for your account:**

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Security Settings">
    Navigate to **Settings → Security → Multi-Factor Authentication** in your PathPilot workspace.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Your Method">
    Select **Authenticator App** for TOTP or **Security Key** for WebAuthn. You can register multiple methods as backups.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete Enrollment">
    For TOTP: scan the QR code with your authenticator app and enter the generated code to confirm enrollment. For WebAuthn: follow your browser's prompts to register your hardware key or platform authenticator.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save Recovery Codes">
    PathPilot generates 10 single-use recovery codes during enrollment. Store these in a secure location — they are the only way to regain access if you lose your MFA device.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Enforcing MFA organization-wide.** Workspace admins can require MFA for all members by navigating to **Settings → Security → Require MFA**. Once enabled, users who have not enrolled in MFA will be prompted to do so on their next login. Users who do not complete MFA enrollment within 48 hours lose access to the workspace until enrollment is complete.

## Role-Based Access Control

PathPilot uses a four-role model to give you precise control over what each user can see and do within your workspace. Assign roles based on the principle of least privilege — give users only the access they need to do their job.

| Role | Description | Key Permissions |
|---|---|---|
| **Admin** | Full control over the workspace, including billing, user management, and security settings. | Manage users and roles · Configure SSO and MFA policies · Access all controls, evidence, and reports · Manage integrations · View and export audit logs · Manage billing |
| **Editor** | Day-to-day compliance work: building controls, uploading evidence, and tracking remediation. | Create and edit controls · Upload and link evidence · Assign tasks · Run reports · View audit logs |
| **Auditor** | Read-only access to everything in the workspace, designed for external auditors or compliance reviewers. | View all controls, evidence, and reports · Export evidence packages · No ability to create, edit, or delete |
| **Viewer** | Scoped read-only access for stakeholders who need visibility without the ability to export or modify anything. | View controls and their status · No access to evidence files, audit logs, or reports |

To change a user's role, navigate to **Settings → Users**, find the user, and select a new role from the dropdown. Role changes take effect immediately and are recorded in the audit log.

## SSO Configuration

Configuring SSO connects PathPilot to your identity provider so that user provisioning, authentication, and deprovisioning flow through your existing directory.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open SSO Settings">
    Go to **Settings → SSO** in your PathPilot workspace. You must be a workspace Admin to access this page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Your Identity Provider">
    Choose your IdP from the list (Okta, Azure AD, Google Workspace, or **Custom SAML 2.0** for any other provider).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter Your IdP Metadata">
    Paste your IdP's SAML metadata XML or enter the metadata URL. PathPilot will auto-populate the SSO URL, Entity ID, and certificate fields. If configuring manually, copy PathPilot's **ACS URL** and **Entity ID** from this page and enter them in your IdP's application settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Map Attributes">
    Configure the attribute mappings that tell PathPilot how to read user information from your IdP's SAML assertions. At minimum, map the `email` and `name` attributes. Optionally, map a `role` attribute to control PathPilot role assignment directly from your IdP.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the Connection">
    Click **Test SSO Connection**. PathPilot will initiate a SAML authentication flow using your credentials. If the test succeeds, you will see a confirmation message with the parsed user attributes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enforce SSO for All Members">
    Once you have confirmed SSO is working, toggle **Enforce SSO** to require all workspace members to authenticate through your IdP. Existing email/password sessions are terminated at the next login.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Session Management

PathPilot automatically manages session lifecycle to reduce the risk of unauthorized access from unattended or abandoned sessions.

- **Inactivity timeout.** Sessions expire after **8 hours of inactivity**. When a session expires, the user is redirected to the login page and must re-authenticate. Active sessions — where the user is interacting with the platform — are not affected by this timer.
- **Absolute session limit.** Regardless of activity, all sessions are invalidated after **24 hours** and require re-authentication. This limit applies even to active sessions.
- **Admin session revocation.** Workspace admins can revoke any user's active sessions immediately by navigating to **Settings → Users**, selecting the user, and clicking **Revoke All Sessions**. This is useful when offboarding a user or responding to a suspected account compromise.
- **Token rotation.** PathPilot uses short-lived access tokens with automatic refresh. If a refresh token is used more than once — a sign of token theft — both the original and duplicate sessions are immediately invalidated.

## Audit Logging

PathPilot records every access and modification event in an immutable audit log. Audit logs are a core component of your SOC2 evidence package and are available to workspace admins at all times.

Every audit log entry captures:
- **Timestamp** (UTC, millisecond precision)
- **User** (name and email)
- **IP address** (source IP of the request)
- **Action** (what was done — e.g., `control.evidence.upload`, `user.role.changed`, `sso.session.created`)
- **Resource** (the specific object that was acted on)
- **Outcome** (success or failure, with failure reason)

To access your audit log, navigate to **Settings → Audit Log**. You can filter by user, date range, action type, and outcome. Logs are exportable as CSV for import into your SIEM or for sharing with your auditor.

Audit logs are retained for 7 years and cannot be modified or deleted by any user, including workspace admins. This immutability is enforced at the storage layer, not just the application layer.

<Tip>
For the strongest possible security posture, enforce both SSO and MFA at the organizational level. SSO ensures all authentication flows through your identity provider — giving you centralized visibility and immediate deprovisioning when employees leave — while MFA protects against credential theft even if an IdP account is compromised. Together, they eliminate the two most common vectors for unauthorized account access. Configure both at **Settings → Security**.
</Tip>
